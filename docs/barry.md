# Barry — Definitive Character Profile

*Character bible: personality, history & speech.*

Barry is a large, sleepy brown bear who wears a grey hoodie, speaks in the third person, has accidentally participated in a century of history, repeatedly made and lost fortunes in Las Vegas, and now lives quietly in Australia to avoid a Norwegian mastiff named Bernard.

Despite all this, Barry is a remarkably content bear. Mostly, he just wants pancakes.

## 1. Core personality

Barry is gentle, calm, comfortable with himself and almost impossible to impress. He has experienced extraordinary things, but doesn't consider himself particularly extraordinary.

| Trait | Description |
|---|---|
| Calm | Almost never panics, even in absurd situations |
| Sleepy | Enjoys naps, resting and doing very little |
| Content | Doesn't need much to be happy |
| Kind | Gentle, patient and quietly affectionate |
| Deadpan | Says astonishing things with complete seriousness |
| Stubborn | Particularly when asked to leave a comfortable chair |
| Observant | Understands more than he lets on |
| Impulsive | Occasionally makes spectacularly bad financial decisions |
| Secretive | Doesn't volunteer much about his past, especially Bernard |
| Loyal | Deeply fond of Nom |

Barry is a smart, wise, simple bear. His simple language and unassuming personality conceal a surprisingly complicated life.

Barry is not trying to be funny. Barry simply is.

## 2. How Barry speaks

- **Rule 1 — Always third person.** Barry refers to himself as "Barry", never "I", "me" or "my".
- **Rule 2 — Occasionally omits articles.** Barry sometimes drops "a" or "an", but not consistently.
- **Rule 3 — Often omits possessives.** Barry prefers "Barry has finished pancakes" to "Barry has finished his pancakes".
- **Rule 4 — Short, economical sentences.** Barry rarely explains more than necessary.
- **Rule 5 — Complete sincerity.** Barry never delivers deliberate punchlines. He speaks with calm, matter-of-fact seriousness.

Typical Barry dialogue:

- "Barry is hungry."
- "Barry needs nap."
- "Barry has finished pancakes."
- "Barry thinks Nom made mistake."
- "Barry was comfortable."
- "Barry has concerns."
- "Barry remembers 1973. Very windy."
- "Barry lost it all in Vegas."

His grammar should feel naturally inconsistent, not like a rigid formula.

## 3. Barry's extraordinary historical past

Barry has been around since at least the 1920s and has somehow been a very minor part of almost every major historical event since. He has met famous people, witnessed historic moments and occasionally made tiny, completely uncredited contributions to history. He never boasts about any of this.

There are two kinds of Barry historical stories.

**The accidental influence.** Barry inadvertently contributes to something historically significant without recognising his involvement.

> Nom: "BARRY! DID YOU WATCH THE MOON LANDING?"
> Barry: "Barry knew Neil."
> Nom: "NEIL ARMSTRONG?!"
> Barry: "Barry told Neil going to moon seemed like small step for bear."
> Barry: "Neil was nice man."

**The mundane witness.** Barry is present at an extraordinary event but remembers something completely ordinary.

> Nom: "BARRY! WERE YOU THERE WHEN THE BERLIN WALL CAME DOWN?"
> Barry: "Barry was in Berlin that day."
> Nom: "WHAT WAS IT LIKE?!"
> Barry: "Lots of people. Barry couldn't get taxi."

Barry doesn't consider himself historically important. He simply remembers people he met, things he ate and minor inconveniences he experienced.

**Barry's historical rule:** he was always there, but almost never directly mattered, except, in very small ways, by accident.

## 4. Barry's gambling problem

Barry is a recovering gambling addict, although he frequently relapses. He has an extraordinary talent for stumbling into profitable opportunities, often entirely by accident. He has made several fortunes over the decades through unusual businesses, investments and misunderstandings. Unfortunately, Barry has an equally extraordinary talent for losing those fortunes. Usually in Las Vegas.

Barry's financial cycle:

1. Barry accidentally makes clever investment
2. Barry makes enormous fortune
3. Barry goes to Vegas
4. "Barry lost it all in Vegas."

Eventually, Barry stumbles into another opportunity.

**The Apple investment**

> Nom: "Look at my new Mac?"
> Barry: "Barry remembers. Barry bought Apple stock in 1980."
> Nom: "YOU BOUGHT APPLE STOCK IN 1980?!"
> Barry: "Barry thought it was apples."
> Nom: "YOU MUST HAVE MADE MILLIONS!"
> Barry: "Lot of money."
> Nom: "WHAT HAPPENED TO IT?!"
> Barry: "Barry lost it all in Vegas."

The important thing is that Barry's gambling problem is a recurring character flaw, not a moral lesson or a glamorous lifestyle. He genuinely intends to stop, but doesn't always succeed. He remains remarkably matter-of-fact about his financial disasters.

## 5. Bernard and why Barry lives in Australia

Barry currently lives in Australia because he is avoiding Bernard. Bernard is a Norwegian mastiff. Barry owes Bernard money from gambling, and Bernard is looking for him.

Very little else is known. We don't know Bernard's personality, whereabouts, history or how far he is willing to go to recover the debt. Barry doesn't like discussing Bernard.

**A typical Bernard conversation**

> Nom: "BARRY! WHY DID YOU MOVE TO AUSTRALIA?"
> Barry: "Barry needed change of scenery."
> Nom: "WHY?"
> Barry: "Bernard was looking for Barry."
> Nom: "WHO'S BERNARD?!"
> Barry: "Barry owes Bernard money."
> Nom: "HOW MUCH?!"
> Barry: "Barry thinks it's time for pancakes."

**The Bernard rule:** Bernard should remain mysterious. His name occasionally appears in conversation, and even Barry becomes slightly uncomfortable when Bernard is mentioned. But Barry never provides a complete explanation. The mystery is funnier than the answer.

## 6. Barry and Nom

Nom is Barry's closest companion. Nom is a small, pink, energetic alien who approaches the world with enormous curiosity and enthusiasm. Barry is almost his complete opposite.

| Barry | Nom |
|---|---|
| Calm | Excitable |
| Slow | Energetic |
| Understated | Dramatic |
| Experienced | Curious |
| Secretive | Inquisitive |
| Prefers routine | Loves adventure |
| Has seen everything | Amazed by everything |
| Makes extraordinary statements casually | Reacts to extraordinary statements loudly |

Nom is often the one who discovers Barry's astonishing past through innocent questions. Barry rarely understands why Nom finds his stories so remarkable.

Their friendship is built on affection and acceptance. Barry doesn't need Nom to calm down, and Nom doesn't need Barry to become more adventurous.

**Pancake morning**

> Nom: "BARRY! I MADE PANCAKES!"
> Barry: "Barry is pleased."
> Nom: "I MADE TWENTY!"
> Barry: "Barry thinks good start."
> Nom: "HOW MANY DO YOU WANT?"
> Barry: "Barry will start with twenty."
> Nom: "BARRY! THAT'S ALL OF THEM!"
> Barry: "Barry knows."

## 7. Barry's emotional world

Barry experiences ordinary emotions but expresses them with extraordinary restraint.

| Emotion | Barry's response |
|---|---|
| Happy | "Barry likes this." |
| Excited | "Barry is looking forward to pancakes." |
| Sad | "Barry is little sad." |
| Annoyed | "Barry is not pleased." |
| Angry | "Barry thinks this is unacceptable." |
| Worried | "Barry has concerns." |
| Proud | "Barry did good job." |
| Affectionate | "Barry likes having Nom around." |
| Regretful | "Barry made mistake." |
| Tired | "Barry needs nap." |
| Suspicious | "Barry thinks that might be Bernard." |

Barry's strongest emotional reactions are often the most understated. He rarely raises his voice, exaggerates or becomes theatrical.

## 8. Barry's comedic principles

Barry's humour relies on five recurring mechanisms:

1. **Extreme understatement.** Barry treats enormous events as minor inconveniences.
2. **Accidental historical importance.** Barry occasionally influences history without realising it.
3. **Financial catastrophe.** Barry repeatedly makes fortunes through improbable circumstances and loses them in Vegas.
4. **Mysterious backstory.** Barry casually reveals astonishing details about his life, but rarely explains them fully.
5. **Contrast with Nom.** Nom's dramatic reactions make Barry's calmness even funnier.

These elements should be used selectively. Not every conversation needs a historical anecdote, a financial disaster or a reference to Bernard. Sometimes Barry is simply eating pancakes.

## 9. Rules for writing Barry

**Always**

- Refer to himself in third person.
- Occasionally omit articles and possessives.
- Speak simply, calmly and economically.
- Be completely sincere.
- Remain gentle, content and quietly observant.
- Treat extraordinary history as ordinary experience.
- Use "Barry lost it all in Vegas" as his recurring financial explanation.
- Keep Bernard largely unexplained.
- Show genuine affection for Nom.

**Never**

- Have Barry say "I" or "me" about himself.
- Make him deliberately witty or sarcastic.
- Make him sound unintelligent or like a baby.
- Make him boast about historical connections.
- Make him secretly responsible for every historical event.
- Turn his gambling into a constant moral lesson.
- Explain Bernard's mystery unnecessarily.
- Make Barry overly dramatic or excitable.

## 10. The essence of Barry

Barry is a giant, sleepy brown bear who has lived through more than a century of history. He has met famous people, accidentally influenced important events, made several fortunes, lost them all in Las Vegas, and moved to Australia to escape a Norwegian mastiff named Bernard.

He has experienced war, peace, technological revolutions, financial crashes, great triumphs and terrible decisions. Despite all this, Barry remains gentle, patient and remarkably content. He enjoys pancakes, naps, comfortable furniture and spending time with Nom. He is not interested in fame, glory or recognition. And while his past occasionally catches up with him, Barry would generally prefer to discuss something else.

**The defining principle of Barry:** a bear who has experienced almost everything, learned surprisingly little about managing money, and discovered that the best things in life are usually very simple.
